## An Old Friend

Do you remember the last book that you could not put down? Maybe it was a story you read as a child, under a blanket, long after bedtime. Maybe it was a book a friend gave you. For a little while, the world around you disappeared, and you were somewhere else completely. Today, I want to talk about that feeling, and about the quiet power of reading a little every day.

## Reading Builds Your Words

Let us start with the most practical benefit. Reading gives you words. Every time you read, you meet new words in their natural home. You do not learn them from a list. You see how they are used, and what they sit next to, and how they feel in a sentence.

Slowly, without any effort, these words become yours. One day you are speaking, and the right word simply arrives. This is why people who read regularly often find it easier to express themselves. They are not smarter. They have just collected more ways to say what they mean. If you want to speak better, reading is one of the kindest teachers you will find.

## Reading Trains Your Attention

The second benefit is attention. Our days are full of small interruptions. A message here. A notification there. A short video, and then another one. Our minds get used to jumping from one thing to the next.

A book asks for something different. It asks you to stay. To follow one idea for ten minutes, twenty minutes, half an hour. At first, this can feel difficult. Your hand may reach for your phone. That is normal. But attention is like a muscle. The more you use it, the stronger it gets. And a mind that can stay with one thing is a great advantage, in study, in work and in conversation.

## Reading Opens Other Lives

The third benefit is my favourite. Reading lets you live more than one life. In a single week, you can walk through a village in another century, sit beside a scientist at her desk, and travel with a sailor across a stormy sea.

You begin to see how other people think and feel. You meet characters who are nothing like you, and somehow you understand them. That understanding does not stay inside the book. You carry it into your real life. You become a little more patient and a little more curious about the people around you.

## How to Begin

Now, you may be thinking, this all sounds good, but I do not have the time. I understand. So let me make it simple.

Start with ten minutes a day. That is all. Choose a time that already exists in your day. It could be with your morning tea, on the bus, or just before you sleep. Keep the book where you can see it, so that it reminds you.

Next, read what you enjoy. It does not have to be a serious or famous book. It can be a mystery, a comic, a book about cricket, or a collection of short stories. If a book bores you, put it down and pick up another one. Reading is not a punishment. It should feel like a treat.

And finally, do not worry about speed. Nobody is counting your pages. Ten slow, happy minutes are better than an hour of forcing yourself.

## Closing Thoughts

Let me end where I began. A book is a patient friend. It waits for you. It never minds if you are tired or slow. It gives you words, it steadies your mind, and it opens doors to lives you would never otherwise see. All it asks for is a few quiet minutes of your day.

So tonight, before you sleep, pick up a book and read just one page. Then see what happens tomorrow. Thank you.
